The real device

Overview

Hello, shake me is a small, battery-powered gadget: a round screen that writes "hello" and, when you give it three quick shakes, shows a kind message with its own little animated scene and a short melody. After a few seconds it fades back to hello.

No app, no account, no internet. It is a tiny object whose only job is to say something kind.

How It Works

The hello screen

A pen writes "hello" in a handwritten style, two butterflies land on the letters, and "shake me" fades in and wiggles now and then to invite you.

Shake

Three quick shakes play the next of five kindness messages. Each has its own animated scene and a short synthesized melody that lasts as long as the message is shown.

Return

The screen fades back to hello after a few seconds, or the on-board button returns you right away. Shakes during a message are ignored.

The Five Messages

  • Your presence is a present to the world.
  • Hey stranger! You are so loved, and you make the world a better place.
  • I'm proud of you!
  • Be gentle with yourself. You're doing better than you think.
  • Even the longest night ends with a sunrise. Keep going.

Hardware

Built on the Waveshare ESP32-S3-Touch-AMOLED-1.75C board.

  • ProcessorESP32-S3R8, 8 MB PSRAM, 32 MB flash
  • Display466×466 round AMOLED (CO5300)
  • MotionQMI8658 accelerometer for shake detection
  • AudioES8311 codec with speaker
  • PowerAXP2101 power management, battery-ready

Under the Hood

Custom 2D renderer. A hand-written renderer (g2d) draws anti-aliased paths, curves, transparency, and glows into a PSRAM frame buffer, with no GPU.

Fonts. A small Python tool converts open-source fonts into compact glyph tables that fit on the device.

Audio. A bell synthesizer runs on the second CPU core, mixing up to 24 voices at 16 kHz and streaming them over I2S.

Shake detection. A dedicated task watches for three jolts above 1.2 g within one second, so everyday bumps are ignored.

Performance. An optional display-library patch cuts full-frame time from 46 ms to 34 ms for smoother animation.

Ideas & Roadmap

The object sits at a nice intersection of hardware, HCI, and wellbeing — a greeting device, a small companion against loneliness, and a platform for studying how simple, kind interactions land.

Still on the list:

  • custom messages sent over Bluetooth
  • more languages, including right-to-left scripts
  • deep sleep for much longer battery life
